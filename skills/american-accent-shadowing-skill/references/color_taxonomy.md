## 2. In-Depth Linguistic Profiles

### 🟡 Slang & Street Talk (`#FEF08A` / Amber-Yellow)
- **Why Shadow This:** Textbook English sounds formal, stiff, and robotic. Native American speakers—especially in modern YouTube videos, podcasts, and casual tech discussions—frequently inject street idioms and modern colloquial expressions.
- **Delivery Tip:** Deliver with relaxed jaw tension and effortless swagger. Never over-enunciate slang; it should flow nonchalantly.

### 🟣 Native Expressions & Idioms (`#E9D5FF` / Purple-Lavender)
- **Why Shadow This:** Non-native speakers often translate thoughts literally from their native tongue. Shadowing native idioms builds "chunked memory" so you speak in complete native phrases rather than isolated words.
- **Delivery Tip:** Treat the entire multi-word expression as a single compound lexical unit. Keep the tempo uniform throughout the phrase.

### 🔵 Connected Speech & Phonetic Reductions (`#BAE6FD` / Blue-Cyan)
- **Why Shadow This:** American English is a **stress-timed language**. Unstressed syllables are squashed, linked, or deleted. If you pronounce every letter distinctly, you sound like a text-to-speech engine.
- **Key Phonetic Phenomena:**
  - **Flap T / D:** When `/t/` or `/d/` falls between two vowels (e.g., *"water"*, *"better"*, *"city"*), it becomes a quick tap of the tongue against the alveolar ridge (`[ɾ]`).
  - **Glottal Stop:** Before `/n/` or at the end of syllables (e.g., *"button"*, *"curtain"*, *"fitness"*), the airflow stops abruptly in the throat (`[ʔ]`).
  - **Vowel Reductions to Schwa (`ə`):** Unstressed words like *"to"*, *"for"*, *"and"*, *"at"* become *[tə]*, *[fər]*, *[ən]*, *[ət]*.
  - **Contractions & Assimilation:** *"going to"* -> *"gonna"*, *"want to"* -> *"wanna"*, *"did you"* -> *"didja"*, *"what are you"* -> *"whaddya"*.

### 🟢 Rhythm, Fillers & Discourse Markers (`#A7F3D0` / Mint Green)
- **Why Shadow This:** American speech has a distinct rhythm powered by discourse markers. These words give you cognitive buffer time while speaking and signal your stance to the listener.
- **Delivery Tip:** Don't stress these words. They should be delivered at lower pitch and lower volume, smoothly bridging major thoughts.

### 🟠 Intonation Peaks & Pitch Accents (`#FED7AA` / Peach Orange)
- **Why Shadow This:** American English utilizes wide pitch swings (melodic variation). Flat pitch sounds monotonous, uninterested, or foreign.
- **Delivery Tip:** Push your vocal pitch up by 3-5 musical semitones on the stressed word, followed by a smooth descent.

### 🔴 Transcription Error Corrections (`#FECDD3` / Light Rose)
- **Strict Rule:** **ZERO creative rewriting or grammatical polishing.**
  - If the creator said: *"We ain't done yet"*, do NOT change it to *"We are not done yet"*. Keep the creator's exact words!
  - If YouTube's AI caption mistakenly wrote *"we eight done yet"* because of background noise, correct it to *"we ain't done yet"*.
  - Mark corrected words with the Light Rose badge so the user knows this was an ASR correction.
